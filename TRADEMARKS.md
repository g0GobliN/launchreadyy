**LaunchReadyy**, **LaunchReadyy Community**, and the LaunchReadyy logos in `public/logo/` identify
the official LaunchReadyy project.

repository. It does not grant permission to use the LaunchReadyy name or logos, and section 6 of
that license says so explicitly: no trademark rights are granted, and reasonable and customary
descriptive use of the project name is all that is permitted by the license itself. This file
explains how the project interprets and applies that rule.

## What the code license lets you do

The software license is the source of truth for the code. Under Apache-2.0 you may:

- Self-host LaunchReadyy Community on infrastructure you control.
- Use it for personal, research, internal, or commercial work.
- Inspect, modify, and redistribute the source, including as a paid or internal service.
- Create and distribute forks, provided you meet the license's notice requirements and keep the
  [NOTICE](NOTICE) and attribution statements.

None of that requires a trademark grant from the project.

## What the trademark policy covers

Trademark rights are about names and branding, not about what the code is allowed to do. Using the
software, including operating it as a hosted service, is permitted by the license — this policy does
not restrict that. What it restricts is presenting your fork, product, or service as the official
LaunchReadyy project.

You are welcome to:

- Say that your project is a "fork of LaunchReadyy", "based on LaunchReadyy", or "compatible with
  LaunchReadyy" when that description is accurate and not misleading.
- Link to this repository and refer to it by name in documentation, articles, and reviews.
- Keep the unmodified name and logo inside a fork you do not distribute publicly.

Please do not:

- Present an unofficial fork, modified version, product, or service as the official LaunchReadyy
  project, or as endorsed, sponsored, or affiliated with it.
- Use the LaunchReadyy name or logo as the name, product mark, or brand of a fork, hosted service,
  domain, app-store listing, or package that you distribute or operate.
- Use the name or logo in a way that creates confusion about who maintains, operates, or supports a
  project or service.
- Register domains, social handles, or package names that imply you are the official project.

Forks and derivative projects should use their own name and branding when distributed to others.
The project name is not a required part of redistributing the code.
